## Chapter 1: Why Reading Accelerates Fluency

Acquiring a second language through **extensive reading** is remarkably effective because vocabulary rarely exists in isolation. When you encounter an unfamiliar expression such as *serendipity*, *ubiquitous*, or *ephemeral* inside a vivid narrative, your brain binds the meaning to the surrounding imagery and emotion.

Consider the subtle difference between memorizing an isolated flashcard and discovering a word organically:

- **Context anchors meaning**: You immediately notice collocations, prepositions, and grammatical patterns.
- **Spaced repetition reinforces recall**: Saving challenging words to your vocabulary book lets you revisit them right before memory fades.
- **Comprehensible input builds intuition**: Over time, complex clauses become effortless to parse without mental translation.

## Chapter 2: Practical Habits for Daily Reading

Try dedicating fifteen uninterrupted minutes each evening to an English essay, technical guide, or classic novel. Whenever you stumble upon a puzzling idiom—for instance, *"reading between the lines"* or *"a double-edged sword"*—simply highlight the whole phrase to see how its figurative sense differs from the literal words.
